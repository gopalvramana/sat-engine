## 3. Who Actually Pays

**Critical insight: the customer is the parent, not the student.** This is unlike GRE/GMAT where adults pay for themselves. SAT parents have higher willingness to pay and want demonstrable results.

### Income Segmentation
| Household Income | Avg SAT Score | Typical Prep Spend |
|-----------------|---------------|-------------------|
| <$55,667 (bottom 20%) | ~887 | $0–$200 (mostly free) |
| $55K–$117K (middle) | ~1,020 | $100–$600 (books, online) |
| >$117,610 (top 20%) | ~1,152 | $1,000–$9,000+ (tutors, courses) |

- High-income families spend up to **$9,000/year** on SAT prep
- Middle-income families: **$100–$600** range — this is our price ceiling to win them
- Families now spend ~18% of household income on academic help (up from 12% pre-pandemic) — Care.com 2025

### Target Customer
Middle-income parents ($55K–$150K household income) who want better results than Khan Academy but cannot afford $1,200 Princeton Review courses. This is a large, underserved segment.

**Optimal pricing for this segment: $25–35/month or $149–$199 one-time.**

## 4. Digital SAT — The Structural Tailwind

### What Changed in 2024
- March 2024: College Board completed transition to **fully digital, adaptive SAT** in the US
- Test is now ~2h 14min (down from 3h); section-adaptive (Module 1 performance gates Module 2 difficulty)
- Delivered on laptop via Bluebook app; no paper

### Why This Helps AI-Native Products
The new SAT is section-adaptive. If a prep product isn't genuinely adaptive, it doesn't simulate the real test. Most incumbents retrofitted old paper content. A ground-up adaptive AI engine is technically superior.

"Few test-prep companies can accurately match the difficulty level required for adaptive SAT practice tests" — this is a real moat.

### Test-Optional Reversal (Major Tailwind)
Universities re-instating SAT requirements as of 2025:
- Yale, Brown, UPenn, Dartmouth, UT Austin

SAT matters *more* now, not less. Willingness to pay is increasing.

## 6. The Churn Problem

EdTech has the highest churn of any SaaS vertical:
- Monthly churn: 5–9.6%
- Annual churn: 50–70%

**Our churn is structural** — students stop after their test date by definition. Average usage window: 3–6 months.

### Three Ways to Fight It
1. **Price as one-time** ($149–$199) — extract full LTV upfront, eliminate monthly churn math
2. **Extend the student journey** — PSAT → SAT → ACT → AP across 2–3 years of high school (3–4x LTV per student)
3. **B2B channel** — sell to tutoring centers or schools; annual contracts mean zero churn problem

### Unit Economics
| Metric | EdTech B2C Benchmark |
|--------|---------------------|
| Monthly churn | 5–9.6% |
| CAC (organic/content) | $10–$50 |
| CAC (paid ads) | $80–$200 |
| Target LTV:CAC ratio | 3:1 minimum |
| Average ARPU | $10–$40/month |
| Payback period | 6–12 months |

## 9. Bottom Line

**The opportunity is real.** The $20–40/month AI SAT prep slot is uncrowded. The digital SAT's adaptive format structurally favors AI-native products. The test-optional reversal increases willingness to pay. India's JEE market is a high-upside second bet.

**The risks are real too.** Structural churn (students leave after test), Khan Academy as free competitor, and the need for measurable score improvement to retain users.

**What determines which path you're on:** Score improvement data. If students can prove they improved by 100–150 points using Aptify, that becomes the marketing, the retention, and the moat. Every product decision should point toward making that outcome reliable.
